My story / The person behind DevFoundy

I’m Kenta.
A student with a passion for building.

IGCSE student, programmer, and the person behind DevFoundy. Here’s a little more about me and the things I put my energy into.

Student·Independent builder·Tutoring co-founder

A student, and always learning

I’m an IGCSE student with a passion for programming. Studying is one part of my life; building projects is another. DevFoundy is where I bring that creative side together and share what I’m working on.

I’m still learning and figuring things out. My projects give me a practical way to explore ideas, work through problems, and create something I can actually use.

Why programming matters to me

What draws me to programming is the ability to turn an idea into something real. A thought can become an interface, a tool, or a whole project. I like having a way to explore a problem by building a possible answer.

That interest runs through my work. JARVIS explores what a personal assistant can be. StageWrite Studio brings together songwriting and software. KineticForm focuses on planning and tracking workouts. Midpoint tackles the everyday problem of finding a fair place for people to meet.

They’re different projects, but each gives me a reason to learn something and put it into practice.

The interests behind the projects

My projects also reflect interests beyond code. Music and fitness both have a place in what I’m making, and I enjoy exploring how software can support those parts of life.

Music & creativity

StageWrite Studio is the clearest connection between my interest in music and my programming work: a space to explore chords, arrangements, and songwriting ideas.

Fitness & progress

KineticForm brings fitness into my project work, with tools for planning workouts, recording training, and looking back at progress.

Programming itself is a creative interest for me, too. Working on my own projects gives me room to experiment and decide what I want to make next.

Building independently, and with others

Alongside my personal projects, I build custom websites. It’s a way to take someone’s idea, business, or work and give it a place online.

I also co-founded a tutoring website. That venture connects the education side of my life with my interest in creating things and working with others.

DevFoundy brings those different threads together: student life, personal projects, custom website work, and an interest in making useful developer resources.

What comes next

I want to keep improving my projects, exploring new ideas, and sharing what I learn along the way. The DevFoundy journal is where I’ll write about that process, including the decisions, mistakes, and small discoveries behind the work.

I’m still at the beginning of my story.

For now, I’m learning, building, and seeing where the next idea takes me.
