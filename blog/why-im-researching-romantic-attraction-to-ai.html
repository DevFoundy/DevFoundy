Why I’m Researching Romantic Attraction to AI

An upcoming research project about the human side of AI: why a conversation with something artificial can still feel personal.

Intouch Kenta TakizawaNovember 13, 20263 min read

Most of my projects begin with something I want to build. This one begins with something I want to understand: why might someone develop romantic feelings toward an AI when they already know it is artificial?

I’m working on a research project called The Psychology of Human AI Romantic Attraction: Loneliness, Anthropomorphism, and Emotional Attachment. It brings together two interests I usually explore separately: technology and psychology. Instead of focusing only on what an AI can do, I want to look at how a person experiences the interaction.

The question behind the paper

A chatbot can be useful for programming, studying, or organising an idea. But the same conversational format also raises questions about comfort, familiarity, and feeling understood. I’m interested in how those experiences might become personally meaningful, and why they might develop differently for different users.

For this project, the focus is the person’s feelings. A human reporting a connection does not establish that the AI has emotions or returns those feelings. Keeping those two things separate helps me ask a clearer question about the human experience.

I don’t want to begin with a stereotype

One possible explanation is loneliness. I want to investigate that possibility, but I don’t want to treat it as an answer before collecting evidence. Assuming that everyone attracted to AI must be lonely would build the conclusion into the question.

My framework also considers how human-like someone perceives an AI to be, whether its responses seem empathetic, and whether it has become emotionally significant to them. Curiosity, a particular persona, or a preferred communication style are alternative explanations I want to leave room for.

A project in two parts

Paper I is the theoretical stage. I’m reviewing existing work, separating the main concepts, and developing five hypotheses. It does not present original participant results. Paper II is the planned survey stage, where I intend to examine whether the proposed relationships appear in participants’ responses.

That structure gives me something concrete to work toward without pretending I already know what I will find. The framework should be open to revision, including the possibility that some predictions receive little support.

Why it belongs in DevFoundy

I care about how people use the things we build, including experiences a feature list cannot explain. This project gives me a different way to explore that interest. The next step is to turn a question I find interesting into a study that can be described clearly and evaluated fairly.

I’ll share the progress here as the work develops. For now, this is an introduction to an ongoing project, with the questions still open.
