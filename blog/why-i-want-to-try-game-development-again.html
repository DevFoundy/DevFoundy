Why I Want to Try Game Development Again

I stopped experimenting with Unity and Blender because I found them too complicated. But I still like the idea of making a game of my own.

By KentaNovember 6, 20263 min read

I Didn't Get Very Far the First Time

Before I seriously got into Python or website development, I was interested in making games.

I had already created quite a few things in Scratch, so trying something more advanced seemed like a reasonable next step.

Then I opened Unity and Blender.

And fairly quickly, I realised that knowing what I wanted to make and knowing how to make it were two very different things.

There was so much to understand. I didn't have much programming experience, and I found the tools complicated enough that I eventually stopped trying.

At the time, that was probably the most practical decision. I wasn't making much progress, and I didn't really understand how to move forward.

But I still find game development interesting.

Enjoying Games Makes Me Curious

Most of the time, when I play a game, I'm simply enjoying it.

I'm not sitting there analysing every mechanic or trying to work out how the code is structured. Sometimes I just want to drive a car around or play a first-person shooter.

But occasionally, a game makes me think about the person who created it.

Someone decided how moving should feel. Someone chose what happens when you press a button. Someone had to work out how to explain the game without making the beginning boring.

I think that's a really interesting kind of problem.

You're building something that needs to work, but you're also trying to make it enjoyable. And enjoyable is a much harder thing to define.

A Simple Idea Would Be Enough

One reason I enjoy Dani's work is that it reminds me how entertaining a relatively simple concept can be.

That doesn't mean making a simple game is easy. I certainly wouldn't claim that after how far I got with Unity.

But it does make the idea of returning feel less overwhelming.

If I try again, I don't think I need to begin with an enormous world, a complicated story, or dozens of different systems.

I'd be happy to make something small that feels good to play.

Maybe that means spending more time on how one action feels than on adding another ten features. I don't know exactly what that project would look like yet, and I don't think I need to decide immediately.

I'm Still Interested, Even If It Isn't My Main Focus

Right now, I'm more focused on software and website development.

I enjoy working on interfaces and thinking about how people use the things I build. There are still plenty of programming concepts I want to understand more clearly, too.

So I'm not announcing a game or setting myself a release date.

I just like knowing that this is something I could return to.

Trying a tool, finding it difficult, and leaving it alone for a while doesn't mean you can never enjoy it again. Sometimes you come back with more experience. Sometimes you have a smaller idea. Sometimes you're simply more patient with yourself.

Making games was one of the first ways I tried turning an idea into something other people could interact with.

I'd like to experience that again someday.
