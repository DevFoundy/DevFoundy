Why I'm Slowing Down on Blogging (But Not Stopping)

Why I’m choosing to publish less often, write about what genuinely interests me, and keep blogging as a place to express myself.

Intouch Kenta TakizawaNovember 14, 20265 min read

Over the past few weeks, I've been publishing quite a few articles on DevFoundry. Some were about programming and technology, while others covered education, music, personal experiences, and pretty much anything that came to mind.

However, I've recently decided that I'm going to slow down on how frequently I publish new articles.

And no, I'm not quitting blogging.

Quality Over Quantity

One of the biggest reasons I've decided to slow down is that I'm starting to run out of genuinely interesting topics to write about.

It's not that I've lost interest in writing. I just don't want to keep publishing articles that essentially repeat the same ideas.

Personally, I believe that publishing three genuinely interesting and well-written articles is far better than publishing seven repetitive ones.

I don't want someone to visit DevFoundry, read a few articles, and realise that they're all saying almost the same thing.

I'd rather take my time and write something that actually gives readers a new perspective.

Blogging Has Changed How I Express Myself

Something I didn't expect when I started blogging was how much it would change the way I express my thoughts.

I've realised that writing is one of the easiest ways for me to share my opinions and feelings without worrying too much about being judged.

Of course, my articles are still available for people to read online, but expressing an opinion through writing feels completely different from saying it directly to someone in real life.

When I'm writing, I can take my time, think about what I actually believe, and explain myself without the immediate pressure of a conversation.

And I think that's one of the reasons I've enjoyed blogging so much.

It's become more than just another section of my website.

It's become a place where I can express myself.

I'm Writing for Two Audiences

When I think about who I'm actually writing these articles for, I'd probably say it's around 50% for myself and 50% for my readers.

I enjoy sharing my thoughts with other people, but I also see blogging as a way to document my experiences.

One thing I've noticed is that my English has been improving as I continue writing.

And in the future, I'd love to revisit these articles and see how much I've changed.

Maybe I'll notice how differently I used to think, how much my writing has improved, or even how some of my opinions have completely changed.

Regardless, I think it'll be interesting to look back and remind myself of the effort I put into something I genuinely enjoyed.

So, What's Next?

From now on, I probably won't be publishing articles as frequently as before.

Instead, I'll write whenever something genuinely interests me.

And when I say something, I mean absolutely anything.

It doesn't necessarily have to be about programming, AI, or technology. It could be about something that happened during my day, a random thought, a new experience, or an opinion I've developed.

I don't really want to restrict myself to specific topics.

After all, this is my Founder's Journal, and I want it to reflect who I actually am rather than just what I'm working on.

I'm also not slowing down because I feel pressured or exhausted.

I still enjoy writing. I simply don't think every thought needs to become an article.

To Everyone Who's Been Reading

I believe I've had around 50 or more readers so far.

Now, I know that might not sound like a particularly large audience, but I genuinely appreciate everyone who's taken the time to read something I've written.

Whether you've read one article or several, thank you.

It's quite interesting to think that something I wrote simply because I wanted to express myself has ended up being read by other people.

I'll still be here, and I'll still be writing.

Just perhaps not as often.

One Last Thing

If there's one thing I'd like to leave you with, it's this:

Continue doing what you enjoy, even if you're not particularly good at it.

You don't have to be an amazing writer to start blogging, just as you don't have to be an expert programmer to start developing something.

When I first started writing, I certainly wasn't doing it because I thought I was an incredible writer.

I simply wanted to share my thoughts.

And over time, I started noticing improvements.

I think the same applies to almost anything in life.

You don't necessarily have to be good at something to enjoy doing it, and you certainly don't have to be perfect to continue.

Sometimes, the most important thing is simply that you keep going.

So, this isn't goodbye to blogging. It's just me choosing to write a little less, while hopefully having a little more to say.

Thank you for reading.
