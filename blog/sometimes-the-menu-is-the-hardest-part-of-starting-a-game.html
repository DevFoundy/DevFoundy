Sometimes the Menu Is the Hardest Part of Starting a Game

Before the first level, there's another experience: working out how to get into it.

By KentaOctober 17, 20262 min read

I like a game menu that understands why I've opened the game.

If I'm starting for the first time, help me begin. If I'm coming back, make it easy to continue.

That sounds fairly obvious. But menus can also contain news, events, shops, challenges, rewards, announcements, and several different play modes.

Some of those things may be useful. Together, they can become a lot to sort through before playing anything.

What Should Stand Out?

I think this is a question of emphasis more than the number of available features.

A game can have plenty to explore while still giving the main action a clear place.

If several buttons are competing with animations, labels, and notification markers, I'm left deciding which one actually leads to the experience I came for.

I'd rather the menu give me a starting point and let me explore the other sections when I choose.

Continuing Should Mean Something Clear

A button labelled “Continue” creates an expectation.

Where will it take me? Which save is it using? Am I returning to a campaign or entering a different mode?

A little context can answer those questions without making the screen complicated.

I don't think game menus need to be plain. They can help establish the mood and personality of the game.

But I still want to get through them comfortably.

The menu is part of playing, even if it's a part people hope to spend very little time on. I'd like it to make that first transition into the game feel straightforward.
