A Game's Sound Settings Deserve Their Own Attention

Music, effects, and dialogue don't all need to be controlled by the same slider.

By KentaOctober 13, 20262 min read

Sound can contribute a lot to a game. It can give an action some weight, help explain what's happening, or set the mood of a place.

But I also think being able to adjust it is part of enjoying the experience.

If a game only gives me one volume control, I'm making one decision about several different things.

Maybe I want quieter music while keeping the dialogue clear. Maybe I want to hear the effects without having a menu theme play at the same volume.

Those seem like reasonable choices to make separately.

Let Me Hear What I'm Changing

A slider is more useful when I can understand the result of moving it.

If I'm adjusting dialogue volume while nobody is speaking, I might have to leave the menu, listen, and return several times before finding a setting I like.

A short sample would make that easier.

I wouldn't want every adjustment to trigger a loud demonstration. But having a clear way to preview the different categories seems thoughtful.

The Opening Matters Too

I'd appreciate being able to reach these settings before a game begins its first scene.

The same applies to the volume of the menu itself. It's an odd experience if the first thing I need to do is rush through an introduction to find the control that makes it comfortable.

These details probably aren't what most people would put at the top of a game's description.

Still, they influence how someone settles into the experience.

I like thinking about them because they're a reminder that making something enjoyable also involves letting different people adjust it to suit themselves.
