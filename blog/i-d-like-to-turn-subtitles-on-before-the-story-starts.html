I'd Like to Turn Subtitles On Before the Story Starts

If the opening scene matters, the settings that help people follow it should be available first.

By KentaOctober 14, 20262 min read

Imagine starting a game for the first time.

A scene begins immediately. Characters are speaking, something important is happening, and you realise you'd like subtitles.

But you haven't reached the menu where you can turn them on.

It seems like a small ordering problem. The option exists. It just arrives after the moment when you needed it.

That's the kind of detail I find interesting about designing an introduction.

Let People Prepare

I think a short setup screen can be useful if it gives people choices that affect the experience they're about to have.

Subtitles would be one of those choices. Text size and contrast could matter too.

I wouldn't want to answer a huge questionnaire before playing. But a few relevant settings would be different from asking someone to customise everything at once.

The purpose would be to help them follow what happens next.

The Words Need to Be Usable

Having subtitles available is only the beginning.

Can I read them against the background? Is it clear who's speaking? Do they remain on screen long enough for the amount of text?

I'm not an expert in designing subtitles. But those are questions I'd want to ask if I were creating a story that depended on people understanding dialogue.

I also like the idea of letting someone change their choice later without losing their place.

The introduction might be something a developer has watched hundreds of times. For the person starting the game, it's the first time.

I'd want to give them a fair chance to experience it.
