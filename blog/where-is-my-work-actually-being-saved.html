Where Is My Work Actually Being Saved?

“Saved” should help me understand where my work is and what I can expect when I return.

By KentaOctober 28, 20262 min read

When an application says something is saved, I'd like to know what that means in practice.

Is it stored on the current device? Is it connected to an account? Will it appear if I open the tool somewhere else?

Those are different experiences, even if they all fit underneath the same word.

Local Can Be Useful

I can see the appeal of a tool that works with information on the device I'm already using.

There may be less setup before I can begin. For a small experiment, that could be exactly what I want.

But I'd need the application to explain the limits of that arrangement.

If my work won't automatically appear on another device, say so. If keeping a separate copy is important, make the option visible.

I shouldn't have to discover the storage model by losing track of a project.

An Account Creates Other Expectations

If I'm signed in, I might expect my work to follow me.

That expectation still needs to match what the product actually does. Being signed in doesn't explain whether a particular change has finished synchronising.

A useful status message would tell me enough to understand the current situation.

Use Words That Match the Promise

I don't think every small tool needs a complicated storage system.

What matters to me is that the explanation is accurate and visible at the moments when someone relies on it.

“Saved on this device” tells a different story from “Synced to your account”.

Those few extra words can help someone decide how to look after work they care about.
