Please Let Me See the Price Before I Get Invested

I'd rather understand the cost of a tool while I'm deciding whether it fits what I need.

By KentaOctober 9, 20262 min read

I don't think there's anything wrong with charging for useful software.

Someone has spent time building it, and keeping it available can involve ongoing work. Free and paid tools can both have a place.

What I care about as a user is being able to understand the offer.

The Timing of the Explanation Matters

If a feature is paid, I'd like to know that before I build my whole plan around using it.

Imagine spending time entering information or preparing a project, then discovering that the final step requires an upgrade you weren't expecting.

The price might be perfectly reasonable. But the way it was introduced would still make the experience frustrating.

I'd prefer to make that decision with the relevant information at the beginning.

Tell Me What's Included

Different plans can be difficult to compare when the descriptions are vague.

I'd want to know the practical differences: what I can make, what I can save, and which limits I'm likely to encounter.

If there's a trial, explain what changes when it ends. If a plan has a usage limit, make the unit understandable.

I shouldn't need to guess what an impressive-sounding tier name means for the task I want to do.

Being Clear Is Part of the Product

When thinking about paid work of my own, I want to remember how those decisions feel from the other side.

A clear price doesn't mean everyone will buy something. But it gives them a fair opportunity to decide whether it's useful to them.

I'd rather someone understand the offer and choose it deliberately than reach the end of a process and feel surprised.
