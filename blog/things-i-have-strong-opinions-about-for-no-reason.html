Things I Have Strong Opinions About for No Reason

Seven ordinary things I apparently have a lot to say about, from cafés and cinemas to group projects and rainy days.

By KentaNovember 8, 20266 min read

Seven completely ordinary things I apparently have a lot to say about.

I think everyone has those random opinions that aren't particularly important but that they're somehow willing to spend twenty minutes defending.

I'm no different.

These aren't exactly controversial political arguments or life-changing discoveries. They're just little things I've noticed in everyday life that I have surprisingly strong opinions about.

And the funny thing is, I don't even completely agree with some of these opinions.

Anyway, here are seven of them.

1. Expensive Cafés Aren't Always Worth It

I understand why people like expensive cafés. Some people go for the atmosphere, the interior design, or just the experience of being somewhere nice.

Personally, I don't really care about all that.

As long as the place is clean, spacious, and the food or drinks actually taste good, I'm happy.

I'm not against paying more for something genuinely better. If the coffee tastes amazing or the café is noticeably more comfortable, sure, I'll pay extra.

But paying significantly more just because the café has fancy decorations and looks good in photographs?

Not really my thing.

I came here for something to drink, not to admire the furniture.

2. Watching Movies at Home Is Better Than Going to the Cinema

I understand the appeal of cinemas. Huge screens, surround sound, comfortable seats, and an environment specifically designed for watching movies.

But there's something about the freedom of watching movies at home that I really appreciate.

At a cinema, there's this expectation that you have to silence your notifications, put your phone away, avoid making noise, and concentrate on the screen for the entire movie.

And while that might be exactly what some people want, I can imagine it being stressful for people who prefer a more social experience.

Sometimes people don't even put on a movie because they want to concentrate on every single scene. They just want something playing in the background while they chat with their friends.

You can't exactly do that in a cinema without annoying everyone around you.

Of course, some people genuinely enjoy the quiet, immersive experience, and that's completely fair.

But I like the idea of being able to watch something without having to follow an entire set of unwritten rules.

3. Competitive Gaming Is Fun Until You Start Losing

I actually love competitive gaming.

FPS games, competing against other players, trying to win — I genuinely enjoy all of that.

Sometimes I even compete purely for fun.

But there's one situation that can completely ruin the mood.

Imagine you've had a long day. You finally get some free time, open your favourite game, and decide to relax.

Then you lose.

And lose again.

And again.

At some point, you start wondering why the activity you chose to relieve stress is suddenly creating more of it.

It's especially frustrating when you're not even trying to take the game that seriously. You just want to have fun, but the consecutive losses somehow make that impossible.

I still love competitive gaming, though.

I just wish it would occasionally remember that I'm supposed to be enjoying myself.

4. Group Projects Are Great — If I Can Choose My Group

I actually really like group projects.

Working with friends, discussing ideas, dividing up tasks, and having fun while getting something done? That sounds great to me.

But there's a catch.

I want to choose who I'm working with.

Some teachers prefer assigning groups themselves, and I understand that there are reasons for doing that. But it can make things unnecessarily awkward.

Imagine being placed in a group with someone you've barely spoken to in your entire life.

Now you're expected to communicate, collaborate, and somehow produce an amazing project together.

For more introverted students, I imagine that can be especially uncomfortable.

And then there's the problem of different preferences.

One person wants a minimalistic presentation. Another wants something colourful. Someone else has an entirely different design in mind.

Suddenly, half the project is just trying to agree on what the slides should look like.

I think group projects can be some of the most enjoyable assignments in school.

But sometimes teacher-assigned groups feel like a random matchmaking system, and I'm not sure I always like the results.

5. School Trips Can Be More Exhausting Than School

School trips are supposed to be fun, right?

You get to leave the classroom, go somewhere different, and hopefully experience something interesting.

And I agree. School trips absolutely can be fun.

But it depends on where you're going.

Imagine spending four hours at an art museum when you're not particularly interested in art.

You're walking around, looking at paintings, trying to understand what they're supposed to mean.

Meanwhile, someone who genuinely loves art might be having the best day of their life.

That's the thing about school trips. A destination that sounds amazing to one person might be incredibly boring to another.

I'm sure there's a fascinating meaning behind the painting.

Unfortunately, after several hours, I'm probably more interested in finding somewhere to sit.

Sometimes a normal school day honestly sounds less exhausting.

6. Presentations Are Better Than Written Assignments

Give me the choice between presenting something to a class and writing a long assignment, and I'll probably choose the presentation.

I really don't enjoy writing lengthy assignments.

It's not that I have nothing to say. I just don't think writing is always the best way for me to express what I know.

I'd much rather stand in front of a class and discuss a topic.

With presentations, I can explain my ideas through speaking, organise how I deliver information, and demonstrate my communication skills.

And depending on how the assignment is graded, those presentation skills might actually count towards the result.

That feels like a much better opportunity to show what I can do.

Of course, I understand that writing is an important skill, and I'm not suggesting schools should get rid of essays.

I just think that sometimes being able to explain something clearly out loud deserves as much appreciation as being able to write about it.

Also, not having to write five pages would be a nice bonus.

7. Rainy Days Are Better Than Sunny Days

This might be one of my least controversial opinions, depending on who you ask.

I really like rainy days.

For context, I live in the city and relatively close to my school, so I don't usually have to travel particularly far.

That means rain doesn't affect my daily routine as much as it might affect someone who has a much longer commute.

And I completely understand why those people might disagree with me.

But personally, I love cloudy, rainy weather.

It's less hot, the sunlight isn't as intense, and everything just feels a little more comfortable.

There's also that earthy smell that appears when it rains, which I really like.

Apparently, there's even a word for it: petrichor.

I don't necessarily need it to rain all day. Sometimes I just prefer a day without strong sunshine.

I know rain can cause inconvenience, especially in a busy city, but when it doesn't interfere with my plans, I find it genuinely enjoyable.

Final Thoughts

After writing all of these, I've realised that most of my opinions aren't actually as extreme as they initially sound.

I don't hate expensive cafés. I just want good food and drinks.

I don't hate cinemas. I just appreciate the freedom of watching movies at home.

I love competitive gaming, group projects, and school trips under the right circumstances.

And I definitely prefer presentations and rainy days.

Maybe the real lesson here is that I have strong opinions about things that are almost entirely dependent on the situation.

Or maybe I just enjoy complaining about minor inconveniences.

Either way, thanks for reading my completely unnecessary collection of opinions.
