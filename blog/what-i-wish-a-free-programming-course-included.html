What I Wish a Free Programming Course Included

Free access matters to me, but so does what happens after someone opens the first lesson.

By KentaOctober 2, 20262 min read

There are plenty of places to start learning programming. The part I'm more interested in is what helps someone continue after that first burst of enthusiasm.

I believe useful programming education should be accessible without an expensive subscription. But making a course free is only part of the job.

It still needs to be something people can learn from.

Show Me What I'm Working Towards

Personally, I find a lesson easier to care about when I understand what the idea could help me create.

A short explanation of a concept is useful. Seeing that concept do something in a small project gives it a purpose.

That doesn't mean every lesson needs to end with an entire application. Sometimes changing a colour, checking an input, or displaying a result is enough.

I just want the connection between the explanation and the actual use to be clear.

Leave Some Space to Try

If every instruction tells me exactly what to type, I might finish without knowing which decisions I could have made differently.

I'd like a course to give me a small variation to attempt myself.

Change what the button does. Add another option. Explain why the result changed.

Nothing enormous. Just enough to make me think about what I copied.

Remember That Getting Stuck Is Part of It

An example that works perfectly is helpful until mine doesn't.

I'd appreciate explanations of a few ordinary mistakes, including how to recognise them. That seems much more useful than pretending everyone will get the same result immediately.

Those are the things I want to keep in mind when thinking about educational resources: a clear purpose, room to experiment, and some help when the expected result doesn't appear.

Someone choosing a free course should still get a thoughtful learning experience.
