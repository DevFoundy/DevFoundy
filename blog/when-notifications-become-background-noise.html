When Notifications Become Background Noise

A notification gets my attention. I think an app should have a good reason to ask for it.

By KentaOctober 10, 20262 min read

A notification is a fairly small thing on a screen. But it's also a request to stop paying attention to something else.

That makes it more interesting to me than just another place to display a message.

What deserves to interrupt someone?

Different Messages Have Different Jobs

An update about something I'm waiting for is different from a suggestion to open the app again.

Both might appear in the same place. I wouldn't necessarily want both to have the same level of urgency.

Personally, I'd like some choice about that.

Let me keep the messages that help me follow my own work. Let me turn down the ones that are mainly there to encourage another visit.

Settings Need to Be Understandable

A single switch for every kind of notification seems simple until I want one category and not another.

On the other hand, a page with dozens of unexplained switches can become its own problem.

I'd appreciate a few clear categories with examples of what each one includes.

I also want the choice to stay respected. Turning something off shouldn't feel like a temporary negotiation.

Useful Enough to Notice

I think there's a risk that too many messages make the meaningful ones harder to recognise.

If everything is presented as urgent, I'm left to work out the difference myself.

That's why I'd want to be selective when building notification features. The question wouldn't only be whether I can send a message. It would be whether the person receiving it needs that message at that moment.

Attention is part of someone's day. I'd like the software I make to treat it with some care.
