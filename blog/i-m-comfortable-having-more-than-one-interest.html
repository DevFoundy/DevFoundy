I'm Comfortable Having More Than One Interest

Programming, design, games, and music don't all need to fit into one neatly defined category.

By KentaOctober 21, 20262 min read

I enjoy software development and web design. I also enjoy games, chess, and exploring creative ideas.

I don't think I need to turn those interests into a single identity before I'm allowed to spend time on them.

Sometimes I want to build something. Sometimes I want to understand how something works. Other times I just want to play a game.

Those can be different reasons to do different things.

Curiosity Doesn't Always Arrive in a Straight Line

An interest in games can make development seem interesting. An interest in design can change what I notice about a website.

But the connections don't have to happen every time.

I can enjoy an activity without immediately working out how it contributes to another project.

Actually, I think expecting every interest to justify itself would make exploring feel much less enjoyable.

Focus Doesn't Have to Be Permanent

Of course, having several interests doesn't mean I can give all of them equal attention at once.

If I'm trying to finish a website, that project needs some concentration. If I decide to learn a new tool, there will be things I leave for later.

I just don't see that as a reason to pretend the other interests have disappeared.

They can wait without becoming abandoned parts of my personality.

I like the possibility that something which isn't my main focus now might become interesting again in a different way later.

For the moment, I'm happy to have things I enjoy making, things I enjoy learning, and things I enjoy simply doing.

I don't need all of them to become a job or a finished project.
